# Hi, I’m Josh Powell.

I work where data, operations, and technology meet—the part nobody notices when it is working and everybody notices when it is not.

I’ve spent 15+ years in IT, but not in one neat lane. I have run my own IT consulting business, supported networks and desktops, worked as a technical analyst in pharmacy systems, handled healthcare and pharmacy EDI, and most recently managed enterprise master data at Niterra North America.

That range taught me something simple: the flashy technology is rarely the whole problem. The real trouble usually starts underneath it—in data nobody trusts, ownership nobody defined, or a process that only works because one person knows where all the bodies are buried.

That is the lane I work in.

## The longer version

People usually call the IT guy or the data administrator when something is already wrong. Everyone wants the immediate problem fixed. I want to know what caused it, why the process allowed it, and what will keep it from happening again.

The technology is rarely the problem by itself. More often, it is a process that creates shadow data in offline spreadsheets because the official system is too difficult to use. Sometimes the right tool exists, but nobody was trained to use it properly. Sometimes a better tool is sitting there unused because no one knew it was available. Fixing the visible error without fixing that underlying process just schedules the next support call.

My career started close to the equipment and the people using it. I came up in the era when support could mean replacing the motherboard in the CEO’s laptop or maintaining ISP towers in the middle of the night. I have done both.

Systems support teaches you quickly that a technically correct answer is useless if it does not solve the actual problem. Consulting taught me to explain what was broken without hiding behind jargon. Healthcare technology added a harder requirement: changes had to work without getting in the way of patient care.

From there, I gradually moved away from hardware and deeper into corporate systems and data. I supported large pharmacy go-lives, worked on data-center migrations, established EDI pipelines, and spent years troubleshooting the places where systems, vendors, and real-world operations stopped agreeing with one another.

Most recently, at Niterra North America, I managed worldwide sales, vendor, employee, shipping, customer, and material master data—including more than 1,200 parts—and supported integration with other SAP instances. I also maintained the SharePoint pages supporting those processes.

Master data gets dismissed as cleanup work. It is not. It is the foundation underneath purchasing, manufacturing, reporting, automation, and every AI system expected to make a useful decision.

Bad data does not become smart because you put a better model on top of it. It just fails faster and with more confidence.

## How I work

- Find the root cause, not just the error message.
- Test the assumption before building around it.
- Keep ownership clear and documentation plain.
- Understand the process before automating it.
- Say what failed, not just what made the demo.
- Treat reliable data as infrastructure, not an afterthought.
